Santa Fe, New Mexico

Wednesday, September 23–Monday, September 28, 2026

Basic Info

  • Dates: Wednesday, September 23–Monday, September 28, 2026.
  • Population: Santa Fe had 87,505 residents in the 2020 census; the Santa Fe metropolitan statistical area, which comprises Santa Fe County, had 154,823.
  • Altitude and climate: Downtown sits about 7,199 ft (2,194 m) above sea level. Santa Fe has a sunny, semi-arid high-desert climate with large day–night temperature swings. Late September usually brings afternoons around 70–76°F (21–24°C), crisp nights around 40–46°F (4–8°C), low humidity, and occasional showers. Bring sun protection, water, and layers; acclimatize before strenuous hikes.
  • Holidays: There are no regular U.S. federal or New Mexico state holidays during the stay or the adjacent week. Autumnal equinox falls Tuesday, September 22. Confirm individual schedules around cultural and religious observances.
  • Safety: Violent crime can pose a moderate danger in parts of the city, and property crime is a significant concern. Avoid isolated areas after dark and leave nothing visible in parked vehicles.
  • History: Tewa peoples have lived in the region for centuries. Spanish colonists founded La Villa Real de la Santa Fe de San Francisco de Asís in 1610, making Santa Fe the oldest state capital in the United States; it became New Mexico’s state capital in 1912. Read more on Wikipedia.

Attractions

  • New Mexico History Museum and Palace of the Governors: Exhibits interpret the state’s Indigenous, Spanish, Mexican, and U.S. histories in and beside the landmark 1610 palace. Address: 113 Lincoln Avenue, Santa Fe, NM 87501. Hours: Tu–Su 10–5; closed M; confirm current schedule. Official site | Google Maps.
  • Canyon Road: A walkable half-mile arts district of galleries, studios, adobe architecture, and restaurants. Location: begin at 403 Canyon Road, Santa Fe, NM 87501. Hours: street always open; most galleries roughly M–Sa 10–5 and Su 11–4. District guide | Google Maps.
  • Bandelier National Monument: A nearby National Park Service site protecting ancestral Pueblo dwellings, petroglyphs, and Frijoles Canyon trails. NPS trip planning | Google Maps.

Both packages require advance reservations and are offered at either location, subject to availability; confirm prices when booking.   • Santa Fe River Trail {692}: Yes—Santa Fe has a paved multi-use river walk. The central reach follows the normally shallow Santa Fe River from the historic Eastside through Downtown and Guadalupe/Railyard, while the longer greenway continues west through residential neighborhoods toward Frenchy’s Field and beyond. It is useful both as a neighborhood walk and as a mostly separated cross-town cycling route; expect street crossings, limited shade, and intermittent water in the channel. Location: Santa Fe River Park at Alto Park, 1121 Alto Street, Santa Fe, NM 87501. Hours: daily, dawn–dusk. City river and watershed information | Google Maps.
  • Bradbury Science Museum and Manhattan Project history, Los Alamos {583}: The public museum of Los Alamos National Laboratory explains the laboratory’s origins in the Manhattan Project and presents current nuclear-security, supercomputing, energy, and other research. Pair it with the nearby Manhattan Project National Historical Park visitor center and historic district; laboratory technical areas are not public. Address: 1350 Central Avenue, Los Alamos, NM 87544. Hours: typically Tu–Sa 10–5 and Su–M 1–5; verify before the drive. NPS Los Alamos planning | Google Maps.
  • Shidoni Gallery and Sculpture Garden {433}: A gallery and outdoor sculpture garden in Tesuque, west of town along Bishop’s Lodge Road, with work by regional and nationally known artists. Neighborhoods

  • Plaza and Downtown: The most walkable base for historic sites, museums, restaurants, and shops; the map pin marks Santa Fe Plaza, 63 Lincoln Avenue.
  • Railyard and Guadalupe: A lively, walkable district for contemporary art, the farmers market, breweries, and Rail Runner service. The Santa Fe River Trail runs along the district’s north side and links it with Downtown, the historic Eastside, and residential areas west of St. Francis Drive; the map pin marks 1607 Paseo de Peralta.
  • Canyon Road and Eastside: Adobe-lined lanes and the city’s best-known gallery corridor, an easy walk from the Plaza; the Canyon Road attraction pin marks the district.
  • Museum Hill: A cluster of major museums, gardens, and mountain views about two miles southeast of the Plaza; the map pin marks the Museum of International Folk Art, 706 Camino Lejo.
  • Southside: A diverse dining and shopping area along Cerrillos Road, better explored by car or bus; the map pin marks the Southside Branch Library, 6599 Jaguar Drive.
