Who supplies what

People provide intent, facts, and authority

Three things no amount of reading the repository will supply: what you actually want, facts that live outside it, and permission to spend, grant access, or set policy. The amber labels are how the record keeps that uncertainty visible instead of letting a guess harden into history.

Who supplies what

Agents turn that into reviewable increments

They shape outcomes, weigh alternatives and record the rejected ones, sequence a build, cut it small enough to review, and answer what comes back. What they never do is decide their own work is finished. A pull request is a proposal; merging it is somebody else's act.

How work gets picked up

The sweep finishes work in flight first

Nobody assigns work. A scheduled pass goes over everything live in a fixed order, and finishing beats starting — a run that spends its whole allowance answering review comments and opens nothing new is a complete run.

How work gets picked up

A shared allowance keeps it reviewable

The budget is a boundary, not a target. A run claims branches already open before selecting anything new, and takes the ranking the repository computes rather than rewriting it — an agent free to re-rank its own queue would drift towards the interesting work and away from the finishing work.

When a person is required

Blocker labels decide who can act

The label on a blocked item decides who may unstick it. Exactly one accepts a reasoned proposal from an agent — a judgement call it can research and recommend on. Facts, spending, access, and policy stay with whoever can honestly supply or authorise them, and relabelling does not move that line.

Decks, briefly

Deck changes follow the publishing contract

Ordinary web work with house rules the shared components assume: topic content inside the page's content card, shared libraries for maps, galleries, collapsible topics and footers, and every supplied URL preserved exactly. Build with bash scripts/build.sh after the final source change, then screenshot anything visibly different.

Ken Novak 2026-08-19 · dd00f702c31b

Demos, briefly

A demo stays a standalone example

Self-contained: it owns its pages, assets, and optional prompt history. The build copies it without rewriting the source and generates one contents entry per immediate directory. Demo language stays distinct from deck language, because blurring the words is how a demo ends up half-converted into a deck page.
