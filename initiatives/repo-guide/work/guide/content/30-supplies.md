What's expected of you here is less than you'd think, but nobody else can supply
it.

@figure division-of-labor

Three things only a person can put into the record. What you actually want,
which no amount of reading the repository will reveal. Facts that live outside
it — what a file contains, what something costs, what a service will allow. And
permission for anything that spends money, grants access, or commits to a
policy.

Everything downstream of that is structure, and the agents supply it. They turn
a want into testable outcomes, weigh the alternatives and write down the ones
they rejected, put the work in order, cut it into increments small enough to
review, and answer the comments that come back. The one thing they never do is
decide their own work is finished. A pull request is a proposal; merging it is
somebody else's act.

When you do answer a question the work is stuck on, a focused helper writes it
down: {{skills.answer-decision.summary}} Your answer lands in the decision
record along with what it settles and what it leaves open, so the next
contributor inherits the reasoning and not just the conclusion.
[The working vocabulary and write rules are here](source:AGENTS.md); [the longer
reasoning is here](source:INITIATIVES_VISION.md).

## People supply intent, facts, and authority

Three things no amount of reading the repository will supply: what you actually
want, facts that live outside it, and permission to spend, grant access, or set
policy. The amber labels are how the record keeps that uncertainty visible
instead of letting a guess get written down as settled fact.
