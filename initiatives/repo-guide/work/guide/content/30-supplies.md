Ask what is expected of you here and the honest answer is: less than you would
think, but nobody else can provide it.

@figure division-of-labour

Three things only a person can put into the record. What you actually want,
which no amount of reading the repository will reveal. Facts that live outside
it — what a file contains, what something costs, what a service will allow.
And permission for anything that spends money, grants access, or commits to a
policy.

Everything downstream of that is structure, and the agents supply it. They turn
a wish into testable outcomes, weigh alternatives and write down the ones they
rejected, sequence a build, cut it into increments small enough to review, and
answer the comments that come back. What they never do is decide that their own
work is finished: a pull request is a proposal, and merging it is somebody
else's act.

When you do answer a question the work is stuck on, a focused helper records it:
{{skills.answer-decision.summary}} Your answer lands in the decision record
along with what it settles and what it leaves open, so the next contributor
inherits the reasoning rather than the conclusion alone.
[The working vocabulary and write rules are here](source:AGENTS.md); [the longer
reasoning is here](source:INITIATIVES_VISION.md).

## People provide intent, facts, and authority

Three things no amount of reading the repository will supply: what you actually
want, facts that live outside it, and permission to spend, grant access, or set
policy. The amber labels are how the record keeps that uncertainty visible
instead of letting a guess get recorded as settled fact.
