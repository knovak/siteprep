A blocked item has to say why it is stuck, and the label it uses decides who is
allowed to unstick it.

@figure blocker-triage

The split is between reasoning and authority, and it matters more than it looks.
A judgement call is something an agent can genuinely help with: it can lay out
the options, weigh them, and recommend one, which turns a decision you would
have had to research into a decision you can review in a minute. So exactly one
label accepts a proposed answer.

The rest cannot be helped that way, for different reasons. A fact the agent
cannot observe has to be supplied by someone who can see it. Spending, access,
and policy stay with whoever actually holds that authority. Guessing at any of
them produces a confident answer with nothing behind it, which is worse than an
item that sits there visibly waiting.

Relabelling does not move the line. The question is who can honestly answer it,
and that does not change because the label did.

Even a proposed answer stays blocked until its pull request merges, so nothing
becomes true just because an agent suggested it. Disagreeing is cheap: naming a
different option is enough to redirect the work.

and [the working instructions explain how a recorded answer unblocks later
work](source:AGENTS.md).

## Blocker labels decide who can act

The label on a blocked item decides who may unstick it. Exactly one accepts a
reasoned proposal from an agent — a judgement call it can research and
recommend on. Facts, spending, access, and policy stay with whoever can honestly
supply or authorise them, and relabelling does not move that line.
