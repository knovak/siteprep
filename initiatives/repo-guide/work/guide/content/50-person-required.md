A blocked item has to say why it's stuck, and the label it uses decides who is
allowed to unstick it.

@figure blocker-triage

The split is between reasoning and authority, and it matters more than it looks.
A judgment call is something an agent can genuinely help with: it can lay out
the options, weigh them, and recommend one. That turns a decision I would have
had to research into a decision I can review in a minute. So exactly one label
accepts a proposed answer.

The rest can't be helped that way, for different reasons. A fact the agent can't
observe has to come from somebody who can see it. Spending, access, and policy
stay with whoever actually holds that authority. Guessing at any of them
produces a confident answer with nothing behind it, which is worse than an item
that sits there visibly waiting.

Relabeling doesn't move the line. The question is who can honestly answer it,
and that doesn't change because the label did.

Even a proposed answer stays blocked until its pull request merges, so nothing
becomes true just because an agent suggested it. Disagreeing is cheap: naming a
different option is enough to redirect the work.

and [the working instructions explain how a recorded answer unblocks later
work](source:AGENTS.md).

## Blocker labels decide who can act

The label on a blocked item decides who can clear it. Exactly one takes a
reasoned proposal from an agent — a judgment call it can research and recommend
on. Facts, spending, access, and policy stay with whoever can honestly supply or
authorize them, and relabeling doesn't move that line.
