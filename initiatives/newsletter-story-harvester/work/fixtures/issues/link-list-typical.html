Energy

Spain runs a quarter on solar — Spain generated more electricity from solar than from coal across a full quarter for the first time, and the grid operator says the record was unremarkable to run.

Grid batteries pass 20 GW — Global grid-scale battery capacity passed twenty gigawatts, four years earlier than the 2022 central forecast.

Heat pumps outsell furnaces again — For the third year running, heat pumps outsold gas furnaces in the United States, by a margin that widened in the south.

Offshore wind auction clears — The delayed North Sea auction cleared at a price below the last onshore round, ending eighteen months of speculation that it would fail.

Coal plant closes early — A plant scheduled to run until 2031 will close in March, after its owner failed to find a buyer for the last two units.

Nuclear restart approved — Regulators approved the restart of a reactor idle since 2013, with conditions on cooling water that the operator says it can meet.

Transmission line breaks ground — Construction started on a line that has been in planning for eleven years, after a rule change removed the last of the county-level vetoes.

Solar panel prices fall again — Module prices fell another six per cent, which manufacturers describe as unsustainable and installers describe as January.

Geothermal drilling doubles — Drilling rigs working on geothermal projects doubled year on year, most of them redeployed from oil and gas.

Electric buses reach half the fleet — One large transit agency now runs more electric buses than diesel, three years ahead of its own plan.

Health

Malaria vaccine reaches ten million — Ten million children have now received the malaria vaccine, and the programme reports supply rather than demand as the binding constraint.

Guinea worm cases fall to nine — Nine human cases were recorded worldwide last year, the lowest count since surveillance began.

A cheaper insulin arrives — A biosimilar insulin launched at a fifth of the reference price, and two large insurers moved it to their preferred tier the same week.

Cervical cancer screening changes — Self-collected samples were approved for primary screening, which trials suggest reaches women that clinic-based programmes do not.

Tuberculosis regimen shortens — A four-month regimen matched the six-month standard in a large trial, with fewer people stopping treatment early.

Lead in spices traced — Investigators traced a cluster of childhood lead poisoning to a single grinding facility, and the recall covers eleven brands.

Measles vaccination recovers — Routine measles coverage recovered to its pre-2020 level in most countries, though not in the four with the largest outbreaks.

Sickle cell therapy approved widely — A gene therapy approved in three more countries, with the price still the open question in every one of them.

Hospital infections fall — Bloodstream infections acquired in hospital fell by a fifth across a national network, which the authors attribute to a checklist rather than to a drug.

Snakebite antivenom trial starts — The first trial of a synthetic antivenom began in two countries, targeting a group of snakes whose current treatment is horse-derived and scarce.

Cities

Congestion charge cuts traffic — Traffic entering the zone fell by eleven per cent in the first month, and bus speeds rose enough to change the timetable.

A city bans right on red — Pedestrian injuries at signalised junctions fell in the year after the ban, against a flat trend in neighbouring towns.

Housing permits jump — Permits rose sharply after a zoning reform that legalised three-storey buildings on lots that previously allowed one.

Bike lane network completes — The last gap in a protected network closed, and ridership on the connecting segment doubled within six weeks.

Bus lanes go camera-enforced — Camera enforcement of bus lanes began on four corridors, after a pilot found blocking fell by two thirds.

Vacant office converted — A conversion of an empty office block to two hundred flats completed under a code written specifically to allow it.

Street trees planted at scale — Fifty thousand street trees went in over one season, concentrated in the neighbourhoods that measured hottest.

Water leaks halve — Acoustic sensors across the network cut non-revenue water by half, paying for themselves in under two years.

A pedestrian street stays — A street pedestrianised as a temporary measure was made permanent, after the shops on it opposed removing it.

Night buses return — Night service returned to four routes, funded by a levy that the hospitality trade lobbied for.

Science

A protein structure puzzle falls — A structure that resisted prediction for a decade was solved, and the method generalises to a family of about forty.

Room-temperature claim retracted — The superconductivity claim was retracted by its authors, who published the raw data alongside the retraction.

Ancient genome sequenced — A genome recovered from a two-thousand-year-old tooth settles a long argument about a migration route.

Fusion shot repeats — The ignition result was repeated four times in a row, which is the part that matters and the part that took two years.

Coral spawning restored — Corals raised in a nursery spawned on a restored reef, the first time the whole cycle has closed outside a tank.

Antibiotic found in soil — A new class of antibiotic was isolated from a soil bacterium and works against a resistant organism in mice.

Telescope sees earliest galaxy — The earliest confirmed galaxy is now three hundred million years after the big bang, and it is brighter than models expect.

Plastic-eating enzyme improves — An engineered enzyme broke down bottle plastic to monomers in hours rather than days, at a temperature a plant could run.

Bird flu vaccine for poultry — A poultry vaccine cut transmission enough in field trials that two countries changed their culling policy.

Quantum error rate falls — Logical error rates fell below the physical ones for the first time on a machine of this size.
