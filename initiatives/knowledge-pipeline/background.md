# Background

Researched 2026-08-27, before objectives were drafted. Findings only.

## Already being provided

### [Zotero](https://www.zotero.org/support/quick_start_guide)

Zotero collects publications, web pages, files, and RSS items; its browser connector can capture metadata, PDFs, links, and a local snapshot of a web page. A single item can belong to multiple collections and carry any number of tags, and shared libraries support collaborative source management. [Zotero basics](https://www.zotero.org/support/quick_start_guide) [Collections and tags](https://www.zotero.org/support/collections_and_tags)

The application, library metadata sync, and 300 MB of attachment storage are free; current individual attachment-storage tiers are $20/year for 2 GB, $60/year for 6 GB, and $120/year for unlimited storage. [Zotero storage](https://www.zotero.org/storage)

The reviewed documentation presents collection, organization, annotation, search, collaboration, citation, and export, but not automated importance promotion, mini-narrative construction, or comparison with standing topic documents. [Zotero basics](https://www.zotero.org/support/quick_start_guide)

### [Readwise Reader](https://readwise.io/read)

Reader brings articles, PDFs, EPUBs, email newsletters, RSS feeds, YouTube videos, and other saved material into one reading and annotation system, with browser capture, highlighting, tagging, notes, and synchronization to note-taking tools. It can export documents as CSV, feeds as OPML, uploaded files as a ZIP, and annotations as Markdown. [Reader product and export details](https://readwise.io/read)

After a 30-day trial, Reader is currently included in a Readwise subscription for $9.99/month billed annually or $12.99/month billed monthly. [Reader pricing](https://readwise.io/read)

The reviewed product material stops at capture, reading, annotation, resurfacing, and export; it does not describe a staged quality-selection and standing-document integration workflow. [Reader product details](https://readwise.io/read)

### [Feedly Market Intelligence](https://feedly.com/ai)

Feedly's AI models gather, analyze, and prioritize material about topics, companies, and trends from large numbers of web sources, while team plans add shared AI feeds, collaboration, insight cards, and automated newsletters. [Feedly AI](https://feedly.com/ai) [Market Intelligence pricing](https://feedly.com/market-intelligence/pricing)

The current Standard plan is $1,600/month billed annually for up to ten seats, 100 AI feeds, five newsletter templates, and collaboration; Advanced is $2,400/month billed annually and adds features including Ask AI, trend dashboards, API access, and multilingual AI. [Market Intelligence pricing](https://feedly.com/market-intelligence/pricing)

The reviewed product material covers automated discovery, prioritization, synthesis, and distribution for market intelligence, but not retained transformations across a general-purpose topic pipeline or the later standing-document and reference-archive lifecycle. [Feedly AI](https://feedly.com/ai) [Feedly subscription description](https://feedly.com/legal/Feedly_ESA_20251022.pdf)

No reviewed offering presented the whole wish as one system: each covers portions of harvesting, tagging, prioritization, or downstream reading and sharing, while the proposed sequence of mini narratives, standing documents, human updates, and backward-linked archival remains distinct. [Zotero basics](https://www.zotero.org/support/quick_start_guide) [Reader](https://readwise.io/read) [Feedly AI](https://feedly.com/ai)

## Lessons from similar attempts

### [Knowledge Vault](https://research.google/pubs/knowledge-vault-a-web-scale-approach-to-probabilistic-knowledge-fusion/) and [data fusion](https://research.google/pubs/data-fusion-resolving-conflicts-from-multiple-sources/)

Google's Knowledge Vault combined extractions from text, tables, page structure, and human annotations with prior repositories, using calibrated probabilities because text-based extraction can be noisy. Related data-fusion work found that sources can conflict and can copy from one another, so apparent repetition is not necessarily independent confirmation. [Knowledge Vault paper](https://research.google/pubs/knowledge-vault-a-web-scale-approach-to-probabilistic-knowledge-fusion/) [Data-fusion paper](https://research.google/pubs/data-fusion-resolving-conflicts-from-multiple-sources/)

### [Wikidata's data model](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Help:Data_model) and Wikimedia knowledge integrity

Wikidata allows statements to carry qualifiers, references, and preferred, normal, or deprecated ranks rather than forcing a single unqualified value. [Wikidata data model](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Help:Data_model)

Wikimedia's current knowledge-integrity program still works on citation gaps, non-authoritative references, machine-readable source metadata, vandalism risk, and changes to references across the ecosystem, showing that provenance and source quality remain active maintenance problems even in a mature collaborative knowledge base. [Wikimedia Knowledge Integrity](https://research.wikimedia.org/knowledge-integrity.html)

### [W3C PROV](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-overview/)

The W3C provenance family distinguishes entities, activities, and agents, and defines interoperable ways to express derivation, revision, primary sources, attribution, and links across provenance bundles. Its change model treats different states as separately identified entities connected by events, rather than overwriting one mutable record. [PROV overview](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-overview/) [PROV ontology](https://www.w3.org/TR/2013/PR-prov-o-20130312/) [PROV constraints rationale](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-constraints/)

### [Pocket's shutdown](https://getpocket.com/en/pocket-and-firefox)

Mozilla closed the Pocket read-it-later and discovery service in 2025 after it had helped millions of people save articles. Users had a limited export window, after which their stored data was permanently deleted; Mozilla described the decision as a shift toward projects that better match current browsing habits. [Pocket shutdown notice](https://getpocket.com/en/pocket-and-firefox)

## Questions this raises

- What is the canonical retained object: a reference, a captured source, or an immutable version of every stage artifact?
- Which tagging dimensions use controlled vocabularies, which allow open tags, and how are changes to either represented over time?
- How are copied, syndicated, or mutually dependent sources distinguished from independent corroboration?
- What evidence and review history accompany an AI quality or importance assessment?
- What defines a topic's mini-narrative sequence, and how are one source's assignments to several topics kept consistent?
- What change volume or urgency measures are meaningful when new narratives are compared with a standing document?
- Which parts of a standing document can AI propose, and which acts remain exclusively human-authored or human-approved?
- What source content may be retained legally and technically when a service supplies only a link, changes its terms, or closes?
- What record demonstrates that a mini narrative was incorporated before it moved into the reference archive?
