Peru

Machu Picchu

Suggested window: approximately May 15–June 5, paired with an eight-day Galápagos cruise

Basic Info

  • Population: The protected archaeological site has no resident population. Machupicchu Pueblo (also called Aguas Calientes) is the small gateway settlement below it; Cusco is the principal regional city and air gateway.
  • Altitude and climate: Machu Picchu stands at about 2,430 m (7,970 ft) in a humid subtropical mountain setting. Days are mild, nights are cool, and conditions vary quickly. The Sacred Valley is mostly lower than Cusco, while Cusco is about 3,400 m (11,150 ft). SERNANP recommends April–October and identifies November–March as the rainy season; late May commonly brings greener scenery, clearer views, and more dependable hiking than the wet months. SERNANP’s Machu Picchu guidance.
  • Safety: Crime is a low concern at the archaeological site, but petty theft is a moderate concern on trains, buses, and in crowded areas. Keep valuables secure and use authorized transport.
  • History: The Inca estate was built in the 15th century, probably during the reign of Pachacuti, and abandoned during the Spanish conquest era. Hiram Bingham brought it to wide international attention in 1911. Peru created the Historic Sanctuary in 1981, and UNESCO inscribed it as a mixed cultural and natural World Heritage Site in 1983. Read more on Wikipedia.
  • Planning window: Target approximately May 15–June 5. Because no year is set, exact weekdays, movable religious observances, and event dates cannot yet be fixed; confirm them when booking. Pack sun and rain protection plus warm layers for early starts and evenings.

Best Time with the Galápagos

Bottom line: Late May is the best overall balance. Machu Picchu’s drier season is beginning, while the Galápagos is leaving its warm season: seas are generally still comfortable, snorkeling remains good, and increasing marine productivity supports excellent wildlife viewing. This also avoids Peru’s heaviest July–August crowds.

Suggested Five-to-Seven-Night Peru Plan

  1. Sacred Valley arrival {714}: Fly to Alejandro Velasco Astete International Airport in Cusco, then descend immediately to a lower Sacred Valley base rather than sleeping first in Cusco. Ollantaytambo is a practical base and rail gateway. Airport address: Avenida Velasco Astete, Cusco, Peru. Ollantaytambo location: Plaza de Armas, Ollantaytambo, Cusco, Peru. Airport map | Ollantaytambo map.
  2. Acclimatize in the Sacred Valley {283}: Spend two or three nights exploring gently at lower elevation. Build in rest, hydration, and a light first day; altitude illness can affect anyone, so seek medical advice for significant symptoms rather than pushing through.
  3. Machu Picchu Pueblo overnight {861}: Take the train to Machupicchu Pueblo, sleep there for one night, and visit the ruins the following morning. Location: Plaza Manco Cápac, Machupicchu Pueblo, Cusco, Peru. Google Maps.
  4. Cusco finish {496}: End with two nights in Cusco after acclimatizing lower down. This order makes the altitude progression gentler. Location: Plaza de Armas, Cusco 08002, Peru. Google Maps.

Tickets and Route Strategy

First-visit priority: Choose Circuit 2—the Classic Machu Picchu route—if available. It pairs the familiar overview with a substantive passage through the ruins.

  • Admission is divided among specific circuits and routes; a ticket is not unrestricted access to the entire complex. The official site currently describes three circuits containing ten routes. Review the official circuit descriptions before selecting.
  • Buy through the official Peruvian government ticket platform. Match the timed admission with train and bus plans, carry the required identification, and recheck current rules and permitted items before travel.
  • High-demand routes and times can sell out. Purchase once dates are firm rather than relying on same-day availability, and use only the exact route and time shown on the ticket.

Attractions

  • Machu Picchu Archaeological Site {638}: The terraced Inca citadel combines ceremonial, residential, agricultural, and water-engineering areas within the forested Historic Sanctuary. Visit only along the circuit and entry time on the ticket. Location: Santuario Histórico de Machupicchu, Machupicchu District, Urubamba Province, Cusco, Peru. Hours: timed daytime entry; verify the current slot on the official ticket platform. Official visitor site | Google Maps.
  • Historic Sanctuary of Machupicchu {925}: The surrounding protected area safeguards cloud forest, mountain ecosystems, archaeological remains, and exceptional biodiversity. Its national-protected-area status makes it the nearby nature counterpart to the citadel itself. Location: Machupicchu District, Urubamba Province, Cusco Region, Peru. Hours: access varies by authorized route. SERNANP visitor guidance | Google Maps.
  • Manuel Chávez Ballón Site Museum {347}: A compact archaeological museum explaining excavation, construction, and daily life at Machu Picchu, with an adjoining botanical garden. Address: Puente Ruinas, km 112 of the railway, Machupicchu, Cusco, Peru. Hours: daytime; verify with the Ministry of Culture before visiting. Google Maps.
  • Ollantaytambo Archaeological Park {572}: Monumental Inca terraces and temple remains rise above a living town whose street plan preserves Inca foundations; it fits naturally into Sacred Valley acclimatization. Address: Avenida Ferrocarril, Ollantaytambo, Cusco, Peru. Hours: daytime; current admission is generally through the Cusco tourist ticket, so confirm before arrival. Google Maps.
  • Museo de Arte Precolombino {159}: A Cusco museum presenting pre-Columbian works selected from Lima’s Larco Museum collection in a restored colonial mansion—an art-focused complement to the archaeological sites. Address: Plaza de las Nazarenas 231, Cusco 08002, Peru. Hours: daily, typically 8–10; confirm before visiting. Official museum site | Google Maps.
