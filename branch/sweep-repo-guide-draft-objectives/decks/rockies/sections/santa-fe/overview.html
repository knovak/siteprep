Santa Fe, New Mexico

Monday, September 21–Wednesday, September 30, 2026

Basic Info

  • Dates: Monday, September 21–Wednesday, September 30, 2026.
  • Population: Santa Fe had 87,505 residents in the 2020 census; the Santa Fe metropolitan statistical area, which comprises Santa Fe County, had 154,823.
  • Altitude and climate: Downtown sits about 7,199 ft (2,194 m) above sea level. Santa Fe has a sunny, semi-arid high-desert climate with large day–night temperature swings. Late September usually brings afternoons around 70–76°F (21–24°C), crisp nights around 40–46°F (4–8°C), low humidity, and occasional showers. Bring sun protection, water, and layers; acclimatize before strenuous hikes.
  • Holidays: There are no regular U.S. federal or New Mexico state holidays during the stay or the adjacent week. Autumnal equinox falls Tuesday, September 22. Confirm individual schedules around cultural and religious observances.
  • History: Tewa peoples have lived in the region for centuries. Spanish colonists founded La Villa Real de la Santa Fe de San Francisco de Asís in 1610, making Santa Fe the oldest state capital in the United States; it became New Mexico’s state capital in 1912. Read more on Wikipedia.

Residence

Pueblo Encantado: A quiet high-desert condo community in the Tesuque/Encantado foothills north of Santa Fe, convenient to Rancho Encantado and roughly 15 minutes by car from Santa Fe Plaza. Location: Pueblo Encantado, 17 Pueblo de Cielo, Santa Fe, NM 87506. Google Maps.

Attractions

  • Georgia O’Keeffe Museum: The largest collection of O’Keeffe’s work and archives explores the artist’s life, practice, and relationship with northern New Mexico. Address: 217 Johnson Street, Santa Fe, NM 87501. Hours: daily 10–5; timed tickets recommended. Official site | Google Maps.
  • SITE Santa Fe: A contemporary art museum presenting international exhibitions and public programs in a striking Railyard building. Address: 1606 Paseo de Peralta, Santa Fe, NM 87501. Hours: Th–M 10–5; closed Tu–W; verify exhibition closures. Official site | Google Maps.
  • New Mexico History Museum and Palace of the Governors: Exhibits interpret the state’s Indigenous, Spanish, Mexican, and U.S. histories in and beside the landmark 1610 palace. Address: 113 Lincoln Avenue, Santa Fe, NM 87501. Hours: Tu–Su 10–5; closed M; confirm current schedule. Official site | Google Maps.
  • Canyon Road: A walkable half-mile arts district of galleries, studios, adobe architecture, and restaurants. Location: begin at 403 Canyon Road, Santa Fe, NM 87501. Hours: street always open; most galleries roughly M–Sa 10–5 and Su 11–4. District guide | Google Maps.
  • Bandelier National Monument: A nearby National Park Service site protecting ancestral Pueblo dwellings, petroglyphs, and Frijoles Canyon trails. September access may require a seasonal shuttle; check alerts before departure. Address: 15 Entrance Road, Los Alamos, NM 87544. Hours: park daily dawn–dusk; visitor center typically 9–5. NPS trip planning | Google Maps.

Neighborhoods

  • Plaza and Downtown: The most walkable base for historic sites, museums, restaurants, and shops; the map pin marks Santa Fe Plaza, 63 Lincoln Avenue.
  • Railyard and Guadalupe: A lively, walkable district for contemporary art, the farmers market, breweries, and Rail Runner service; the map pin marks 1607 Paseo de Peralta.
  • Canyon Road and Eastside: Adobe-lined lanes and the city’s best-known gallery corridor, an easy walk from the Plaza; the Canyon Road attraction pin marks the district.
  • Museum Hill: A cluster of major museums, gardens, and mountain views about two miles southeast of the Plaza; the map pin marks the Museum of International Folk Art, 706 Camino Lejo.
  • Southside: A diverse dining and shopping area along Cerrillos Road, better explored by car or bus; the map pin marks the Southside Branch Library, 6599 Jaguar Drive.
