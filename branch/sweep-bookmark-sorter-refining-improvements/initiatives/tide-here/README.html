Tide Here

Tide Here shows five coast-local days of high and low tides, sunrise and sunset, moonrise and moonset, and moon phase for a place or latitude/longitude. It names the resolved place, the chosen coast and the official prediction station. When several nearby coasts are plausible, it shows the closest station first and keeps the other candidates available for review.

The two current Sites are public, separate deployments of the same merged FES2022-capable source:

  • Test: tide-here-test.ken-novak.chatgpt.site version 15.
  • Production: tide-here-five-coast-local-days.ken-novak.chatgpt.site version 7.

Both use direct NOAA and CHS forecasts, licensed Bureau of Meteorology forecasts for all 76 Australian Standard Ports, and the licensed global coastal FES2022b fallback. Official configured sources win whenever Tide Here can make a confident official match. FES is considered only after official coverage declines or the official choices are too distant or ambiguous; those named official choices remain available as alternatives.

NOAA prediction stations cover the United States and its territories, Canadian Hydrographic Service stations cover Canada, and the stored Bureau catalogue covers its 76 Australian Standard Ports. Elsewhere, Tide Here can select a derived FES2022 water point from its sampled global coastline only when that point is within 40 km of the resolved place. A place without a suitable official station or initialized FES point receives a coverage message rather than a prediction borrowed from a distant coast.

Where the tide data lives

The Sites app has no database. Test and production each have a separate private TIDE_DATA R2 object store. Each store holds the active versioned Bureau catalogue plus the derived FES2022 tile index and 376 non-empty harmonic tiles: 377 checksum-addressed JSON objects, 65,203 coastal points, and 146,330,220 bytes. These are runtime data, not publicly downloadable Site assets and not application code.

The complete global package is retained outside Git at ../siteprep-data/tide-here/fes2022/global-coast-r1, so either Site can be reloaded without downloading or extracting the atlas again. Git contains the preparation and verification code plus a small seven-point validation extract, not the global package. The original 3.95-GB FES2022 NetCDF is neither in the Site nor in Git. It is retained outside the repository at ../siteprep-data/tide-here/fes2022/FES2022b_OceanTide_NSgrid.nc for reuse.

Interpreting FES2022 results

A location labelled FES2022 near _place_ is a prediction from the nearest initialized sampled water point, not an official tide station and not a claim that the point is inside that town or harbour. The coastal package was sampled at approximately 15 km intervals and Tide Here will not select a point more than 40 km from the resolved location.

FES2022 is an approximate astronomical harmonic model. It is useful for an estimate of the tide pattern and high/low timing, but it does not include wind, air pressure, river flow, waves, storm surge, local harbour effects, or later observations. Those effects can materially change actual water level and event time. Tide Here therefore marks the source approximate and keeps the general not-for-navigation-or-safety warning; the source details inside the location block are the complete disclosure, with no second FES banner.

FES harmonic amplitudes are in centimetres around the model's mean-sea-level harmonic datum. Tide Here calculates extrema, converts their heights to metres, and groups them into five civil days using the selected model point's IANA time zone. A displayed FES height is therefore metres relative to model mean sea level—not depth, chart datum, lowest astronomical tide, or predicted clearance. Do not compare its absolute height directly with a Bureau, NOAA, CHS, chart, or harbour value unless the datum relationship has been established. The fixed Australian comparison removed one constant datum offset before evaluating timing and tidal shape for exactly this reason.

Use the Site

  1. Enter a place such as Half Moon Bay or Vancouver, or decimal coordinates such as 47.61, -122.33, then choose Show selection. Or choose Show here and approve the browser's one-time location request to use the device's current coordinates.
  2. If several nearby coasts are plausible, Tide Here immediately shows the closest result. Open Alternative coasts below the forecast to choose a different named prediction station.
  3. Read each day's high and low tides. Past tide labels are lighter than the upcoming tides. Open the day's Sun and moon row for sunrise, sunset, moonrise, moonset and phase.
  4. Open the coast name for the original entry, resolved place, station and time zone.
  5. Below the safety notice, open Prediction source details for the provider, station type, units and datum. Alternative coasts, when needed, appears after the results and before Debug record. Open Debug record for local history and a description of what leaves the device.

All headings and events use the selected coast or FES model point's civil time zone, including when the five-day range crosses a daylight-saving change. Heights are metres relative to the datum named in the source details. Predictions are informational and are not for navigation or safety decisions.

Local history and privacy

Tide Here requests browser location only after Show here is chosen. The browser and operating system handle that permission and return coordinates to the page; a denied or unavailable location leaves the manual entry available. Coordinates from Show here, or a place or coordinates submitted manually, go directly from the browser to the configured Nominatim geocoder. The browser requests NOAA and CHS station data and forecasts directly from those providers.

The browser also loads the Australian station catalogue from Tide Here. When an Australian Standard Port is selected, it sends the original display value, resolved place and coast context, selected port, time zone and five-day bounds to the Site's /forecast gateway. If official coverage declines or only distant, ambiguous official choices remain and an active FES2022 artifact is available, the browser sends the coordinates in the body of a POST /resolve request and then sends the selected model point through the same forecast gateway. The official choices remain available as alternatives. Coordinates are not placed in a URL. The gateway reads versioned provider data from its object store. Its operational logs contain only route, method, status, provider and elapsed time—not request bodies, place names, coordinates or station identifiers. Tide Here has no account, analytics service or cloud forecast history, and the object store contains provider data rather than user history.

Up to 100 successful or partial forecast responses are kept in this browser. Open Debug record, then choose Show local history to inspect them, download them as JSON or clear them. Clearing the readable history leaves short-lived provider caches alone. On a public or shared device, clear the history when finished.

Run and verify locally

Install the repository dependencies and run the repository build from the root:

npm ci
npm run build

Then verify the current Tide Here Sites app and its deterministic browser page:

cd initiatives/tide-here/work
npm ci
npm test
npm run build
cd ../../..
./node_modules/.bin/playwright test --config initiatives/tide-here/work/phase-6/playwright.config.mjs

If Chromium has not already been provisioned, run npm run setup:browsers before the Playwright command. To inspect the page without live provider calls, serve the repository root and open the fixture URL:

python3 -m http.server 4179 --bind 127.0.0.1

http://127.0.0.1:4179/initiatives/tide-here/work/phase-6/index.html?fixture=1

Remove ?fixture=1 for a live provider smoke test. Live checks are separate from the gating suite because provider availability and CORS can change.

Deploy

The current Tide Here source is a ChatGPT Sites app. Its initiative.json deployment record points to initiatives/tide-here/work, while .openai/hosting.json binds the object store used for immutable provider registries and tide datasets. The browser still calls NOAA and CHS directly; the Site serves the Australian catalogue and forecasts plus active FES2022 model-point resolution and forecasts. A protected initializer loads and verifies the exact stored data before activating a registry.

Use the repository's deploy-test skill to refresh the test Site. Use release-initiative only when a person explicitly asks to release the committed source to production. Test and production are separate Sites, and replacing either one preserves its URL and access setting. Merging a source or documentation pull request does not deploy either Site.

After a deployment, verify the root page, registry health, one NOAA result such as Half Moon Bay, one CHS result such as Vancouver, representative Australian results with the Bureau source, disclaimer and dataset version, and FES results such as Galway, Cooktown, Gibraltar, Nice, or Amsterdam. Maroochydore and Bundaberg must continue to select their official Bureau ports before FES. Also verify an ambiguous result with its closest forecast and collapsed alternatives, the coast and astronomy disclosures, the safety/source/alternatives/debug order, the privacy/history controls, and a 430×932 phone viewport without horizontal overflow.

For the implementation contract and alternatives, see spec.md. For the current Sites app, stored-provider boundary and recorded test version, see work/phase-13/README.md. For the earlier production-deployment evidence, see work/phase-8/README.md and work/phase-8/evidence.json.
