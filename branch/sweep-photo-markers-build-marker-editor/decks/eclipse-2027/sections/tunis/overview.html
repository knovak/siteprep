Tunis, Tunisia

Friday, July 30–Thursday, August 5, 2027

The medina, Carthage, coastal villages, and a southern excursion for totality.

Basic Info

  • Dates: Friday, July 30–Thursday, August 5, 2027; eclipse day is Monday, August 2.
  • Population: About 693,000 in the central city and roughly 2.9 million in the metropolitan area in the 2024 figures in the city profile. Other definitions of Greater Tunis give about 2.7 million; these boundaries are not interchangeable. City profile | Metropolitan estimates.
  • Altitude and climate: Much of central Tunis is close to sea level, with the old city on a low hill and elevations around 40 m. Late July and early August are hot, sunny, and usually dry: typical highs about 33–35°C (91–95°F), lows about 22–24°C (72–75°F), with hotter spells possible. Visit exposed ruins early. Climate averages.
  • Holidays: Republic Day falls on Sunday, July 25, five days before arrival and within the preceding-week check. No regular national holiday is listed during the stay or August 6–12. National Women’s Day is Friday, August 13, eight days after departure; recheck closures if extending the trip. Lunar holiday dates remain subject to confirmation. 2027 holiday calendar.
  • Crime: Petty theft and bag snatching are concerns in crowded tourist areas and on transport. Secure phones and bags; use reliable transport after dark. Current travel advice.
  • History: Ancient Tunis predates the medieval city; its Islamic medina developed from 698 CE after the Arab conquest. It became a major Hafsid capital in the 13th century, expanded during the French protectorate, and remained the national capital after independence in 1956. History on Wikipedia.

Solar Eclipse — August 2, 2027

Tunis, Carthage, Sidi Bou Said, and La Marsa are outside the totality path. A stay based here needs a separate southern viewing plan to experience a total eclipse. Tunis visibility and the National Solar Observatory path map distinguish the partial and total zones. El Jem and Sfax are mapped below; arrange an overnight move on Sunday, August 1 rather than depending on a long eclipse-morning transfer.

Eye safety: Use ISO 12312-2 eclipse glasses whenever any bright part of the Sun is visible. Remove them only during totality, at a location actually inside the totality path. Cameras, binoculars, and telescopes need suitable filters over the front of the optics. NASA viewing guidance.

Attractions

  • Medina and Zitouna Mosque surroundings {881}: The UNESCO-listed old city’s covered souks and lanes reward an early walk. Mosque interior access is restricted; ask locally about any visitor courtyard opening and dress requirements. Address: Rue Jemaa Ez Zitouna, Medina, Tunis, Tunisia; نهج جامع الزيتونة، المدينة العتيقة، تونس، تونس. Hours: Lanes accessible daily; souk shops mainly daytime, with Friday closures possible. Official tourism guide | Google Maps.
  • Bardo National Museum {280}: A former palace containing an exceptional collection of Roman mosaics and archaeology from across Tunisia. Schedule it on a day other than eclipse Monday. Address: Rue Mongi Slim, Le Bardo 2000, Tunisia; نهج المنجي سليم، باردو ٢٠٠٠، تونس. Hours: Tu–Su 9–5 in summer; M closed. Reconfirm before travel. Visitor information | Google Maps.
  • Carthage — Antonine Baths {369}: Monumental Roman bath ruins beside the sea, part of the archaeological site of ancient Carthage. Use the baths as the map reference; museum restoration and individual site access can differ. Address: Impasse des Thermes d’Antonin, Carthage 2016, Tunisia; نهج حمامات أنطونيوس، قرطاج ٢٠١٦، تونس. Hours: Daytime archaeological-site hours; verify the summer closing time. Heritage agency / Carthage sites | Google Maps.
  • Sidi Bou Said {861}: White-and-blue streets, sea views, and cafés on a hill above the gulf. The village makes a rewarding half-day paired with Carthage; it has only a partial eclipse. Address: Rue Habib Thameur, Sidi Bou Said 2026, Tunisia; نهج الحبيب ثامر، سيدي بوسعيد ٢٠٢٦، تونس. Hours: Public lanes daily; café and gallery hours vary. Official tourism guide | Google Maps.
  • MACAM — National Museum of Modern and Contemporary Art {231}: Tunisian modern and contemporary painting and changing exhibitions inside the Cité de la Culture complex. Address: Cité de la Culture, Avenue Mohamed V, Tunis, Tunisia; مدينة الثقافة، شارع محمد الخامس، تونس، تونس. Hours: Exhibition hours vary; confirm the current program. Museum website | Google Maps.

Neighborhoods

  • Medina: The historic souks and lanes around Zitouna Mosque, with traditional guesthouses and palace courtyards; best explored on foot in daylight.
  • Ville Nouvelle / Avenue Habib Bourguiba: The central boulevard and cafés east of Bab el Bhar; a convenient base for downtown walks and transport. Both the boulevard reference and gate are mapped.
  • Carthage: A spread-out coastal archaeological suburb; combine walking within sites with taxis between them.
  • Sidi Bou Said and La Marsa: Sidi Bou Said offers steep, walkable village lanes; nearby La Marsa offers a beach promenade, cafés, and a more relaxed coastal base. Neither is in totality.

Eclipse-day excursions

  • El Jem — totality and Roman amphitheatre {724}: A practical southern alternative for eclipse day, roughly 200 km from Tunis. The town gets around 4 minutes 25 seconds of totality. Consider sleeping in the totality zone on August 1. The amphitheatre is a landmark reference, with no eclipse admission or viewing reservation implied. Address: Amphitheatre of El Jem, Route de Sfax, El Jem 5160, Tunisia; المدرج الروماني، طريق صفاقس، الجم ٥١٦٠، تونس. Hours: Archaeological site daytime; eclipse-day access must be confirmed. El Jem eclipse circumstances | Google Maps.
  • Sfax — alternative totality base {572}: Farther south, around 270 km from Tunis, Sfax lies in the totality corridor. Choose an open site by its exact coordinates, book accommodation and local transport, and use the forecast to decide between southern bases. Bab Diwan is a city reference, not a confirmed observing site. Address: Bab Diwan, Boulevard Farhat Hached, Sfax 3000, Tunisia; باب الديوان، شارع فرحات حشاد، صفاقس ٣٠٠٠، تونس. Hours: Public streets; any managed viewing site needs separate confirmation. Sfax eclipse circumstances | Google Maps.

Events

  • Partial eclipse in Tunis — Monday, August 2 {329}: Tunis sees a partial eclipse only, roughly 8:56–11:28am, with maximum around 10:10am local time (UTC+1). Keep eclipse glasses on throughout. The central avenue is a reference location, not an organized viewing event. For totality, move south before eclipse morning. Address: Avenue Habib Bourguiba, Tunis, Tunisia; شارع الحبيب بورقيبة، تونس، تونس. Hours: Morning; verify the exact observing location. Tunis eclipse circumstances | Google Maps.
