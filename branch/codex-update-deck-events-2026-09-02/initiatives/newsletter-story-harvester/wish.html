Harvest and re-organize the contents of newsletter emails that contain large numbers of stories. Examples include ‘future crunch’ or ‘fix the news’ or ‘ Americans of conscience ‘ news posts that have a small amount of text with each link. This also includes Stanford energy or Yale newsletters, which contain a moderate amount of text for each link or story. This also includes Substack columns from authors like Yglesias or Roberts, which may have large amounts of text in a single item with embedded links.

So to harvest these different subsets of Messages, we need to search and find the appropriate emails in my Gmail box.

Once harvested, group the stories by content area or theme.  Retain the information about the date associated with each story.

Condense the harvested material from each content area into a nice webpage with drop down controls that allow collapsing or expanding stories. Allow the user to sort stories by date or other parameters.   If stories are closely related, offer paraphrase summaries that combine multiple stories in the single stories. Provide buttons on the page to rapidly dispose of  unwanted stories, or to emphasize ones that are worth holding onto.

Preserve the results in a database for further user processing. Also output HTML to share the emphasized stories or the retained stories.  Consider publication as an openAI site.

This entire process might run through codex skills rather than a freestanding website. That would allow the skills to do some of the harvesting and paraphrasing
