Taos, New Mexico

Wednesday, September 23 and Monday, September 28–Wednesday, September 30, 2026

Basic Info

  • Dates: Wednesday, September 23 and Monday, September 28–Wednesday, September 30, 2026.
  • Population: Taos had 6,474 residents in the 2020 census. The wider Taos micropolitan area, coterminous with Taos County, had 34,489 residents.
  • Altitude and climate: The town sits about 6,969 ft (2,124 m) above sea level. Its sunny, semi-arid mountain climate brings warm days, cool nights, and big temperature swings. Late September typically means afternoons near 68–74°F (20–23°C), nights near 36–42°F (2–6°C), and only occasional rain. Pack layers, sun protection, and water, and allow time to acclimatize.
  • Holidays and observances: There are no regular U.S. federal or New Mexico state holidays during the stay or the adjacent week. The autumnal equinox is Tuesday, September 22. Taos Pueblo traditionally observes San Geronimo Feast Day on September 30; this is a sacred community observance rather than a public holiday, and access rules can change.
  • History: Taos Pueblo has been continuously inhabited for roughly a millennium. Spanish colonists established nearby Don Fernando de Taos in 1795; the town later became a trading, arts, and counterculture center. Read more on Wikipedia.

Attractions

  • Taos Pueblo: A living Tiwa community and UNESCO World Heritage Site whose multi-story adobe buildings are among North America’s oldest continuously inhabited structures. Respect all posted photography and closure rules. Address: 120 Veterans Highway, Taos, NM 87571. Hours: generally daily 9–4; call ahead because ceremonies and community needs can close the pueblo without notice. Official visitor information | Google Maps.
  • Harwood Museum of Art: The University of New Mexico museum spans historic Taos artists, Hispanic devotional art, Indigenous work, and modern and contemporary art, including an Agnes Martin gallery. Address: 238 Ledoux Street, Taos, NM 87571. Hours: W–Su 11–5; verify before visiting. Official visitor information | Google Maps.
  • Rio Grande Gorge Bridge: A dramatic steel bridge and overlook about ten miles northwest of town, with sweeping views into the gorge and accessible rim paths. Location: US–64 at the Rio Grande Gorge, Taos, NM 87529. Hours: overlook and rest area generally daily during daylight. BLM monument information | Google Maps.
  • Kit Carson Home & Museum: An 1825 adobe home interpreting Carson, his family, and the complicated history of the nineteenth-century Southwest. Address: 113 Kit Carson Road, Taos, NM 87571. Hours: M–Sa 10–5, Su 11–4; confirm seasonal hours. Official site | Google Maps.
  • Earthship Visitor Center: A self-guided introduction to off-grid buildings made with passive-solar design and natural and recycled materials. Address: 2 Earthship Way, Tres Piedras, NM 87577. Hours: daily 10–4; reserve and verify online. Official visitor information | Google Maps.

Neighborhoods

  • Taos Plaza and Historic District: The most walkable visitor base, centered on the old plaza and its galleries, shops, restaurants, and adobe lanes. Location: Taos Plaza, 105 Don Fernando Street, Taos, NM 87571. Google Maps.
  • Ledoux Street: A compact, pedestrian-friendly historic arts district just southwest of the Plaza, anchored by the Harwood Museum; use the museum address and map link above.
  • Kit Carson Road: A gallery-lined route east from the Plaza toward museums and Kit Carson Park; the map pin marks Kit Carson Park, 211 Paseo del Pueblo Norte, Taos, NM 87571. Google Maps.
  • Ranchos de Taos: A village south of town known for its historic adobe church and plaza. Location: San Francisco de Asís Church, 60 St Francis Plaza, Ranchos de Taos, NM 87557. Hours: church generally M–F 9–4; worship and events take priority. Google Maps.
