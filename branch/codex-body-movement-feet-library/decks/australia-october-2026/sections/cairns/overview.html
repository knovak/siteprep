Cairns, Queensland

Monday, October 19–Sunday, October 25, 2026

Basic Info

  • Dates: Monday, October 19 through Sunday, October 25, 2026.
  • Population: Cairns had 153,181 residents in its urban area and 166,943 in the wider Cairns Region local government area at the 2021 census. The region is the most useful approximation of the metropolitan population. Australian Bureau of Statistics census profile.
  • Altitude and climate: Central Cairns is about 2 m above sea level. The tropical monsoon climate is warm year-round; late October usually brings daytime highs near 30°C, nights near 21°C, high UV, rising humidity, and occasional showers before the main wet season.
  • Expected weather: Plan for hot, humid days, warm evenings, and intermittent rain. Carry water and sun protection, schedule strenuous outdoor activity early, and keep a light rain layer available.
  • Holidays: No Queensland public holiday falls during the stay or the preceding or following week. Queensland public holiday dates.
  • History: Gimuy (Cairns) is on the traditional lands of the Gimuy Walubara Yidinji and Yirrganydji peoples. The colonial port settlement was founded on October 7, 1876 and named for Queensland governor William Cairns; it grew through gold, agriculture, rail, and tourism. Read more on Wikipedia.
  • Safety: Crime is a moderate concern rather than a general barrier to visiting. Use normal city precautions, secure valuables, avoid isolated areas after dark, and take extra care around nightlife precincts.

Attractions

  • Cairns Art Gallery {284}: The regional gallery presents Australian contemporary art, including work connected to Far North Queensland and First Nations perspectives, in a restored heritage building. Address: 40 Abbott Street, Cairns City QLD 4870, Australia. Hours: M–F 9–5, Sa 10–5, Su 10–2. Official information | Google Maps.
  • Cairns Botanic Gardens {731}: Tropical gardens display rainforest plants, palms, orchids, and species from northern Australia and other tropical regions. Address: 78–96 Collins Avenue, Edge Hill QLD 4870, Australia. Hours: Daily 7:30–5:30. Official information | Google Maps.
  • Cairns Aquarium {496}: Exhibits follow habitats from the Wet Tropics rainforest to the Great Barrier Reef, with freshwater and marine species from across tropical North Queensland. Address: 5 Florence Street, Cairns City QLD 4870, Australia. Hours: Daily 9:30–3:30; last admission 2:30. Official information | Google Maps.
  • Barron Gorge National Park {852}: Wet Tropics rainforest, steep gorge scenery, walking tracks, and lookouts surround Barron Falls near Kuranda. The Din Din Barron Falls lookout is accessible by an elevated rainforest boardwalk. Address: Barron Falls Road, Kuranda QLD 4881, Australia. Hours: Open 24h; visit in daylight. Official park information | Google Maps.

Neighborhoods

  • Cairns City and the Esplanade: The compact, walkable visitor center combines the waterfront lagoon, restaurants, galleries, the marina, and the main departure points for reef trips.
  • Edge Hill: A leafy dining area beside the Botanic Gardens and Tanks Arts Centre, useful as the starting point for the Mount Whitfield walking tracks.
  • Cairns North: A practical inner suburb along the northern Esplanade with waterfront paths, hotels, and easy access to the city center.
  • Kuranda: A rainforest village northwest of Cairns with markets, walks, and rail and cableway connections; treat it as a day-trip center rather than a walk from Cairns.

Address: Tanks Arts Centre, 46 Collins Avenue, Edge Hill QLD 4870, Australia. Hours: Su 9–2. Official market information | Google Maps.
